# Photo → top charm: manual decisions that matter

1. **Choose the subject.** Use your own photo or a source with the required reuse rights. A single recognisable object with a solid lower mass works better than a fragile stem, handle, gap or several disconnected pieces. Crop to the whole object; keep the background plain. Do not use a competitor's product photo to recreate their mesh.
2. **Generate the raw 3D asset.** Use your chosen image-to-3D tool and export GLB, OBJ or STL. The original project used Microsoft's TRELLIS for some image-to-3D work, but its installation, GPU, model terms and output quality belong to that project. Save the input photo, tool/model version, licence and original output as your private provenance record.
3. **Clean the mesh in Blender.** Remove unintended objects and floating islands. For a 3D print, make the visible parts real joined geometry, close holes, give thin features enough thickness for your nozzle and material, and preserve the subject's identity. A voxel remesh can close holes but can also erase detail or create brittle films. Check the result from below as well as above.
4. **Choose the connector seat.** The diamond plug needs solid body mass above it. A default centre position fails for hollow, arch, stem or handle subjects. Move/rebuild the body or connector in Blender when needed. Check that no helper riser reads as an alien pedestal on the finished object. Connector clearance is a starting point; test fit is physical evidence.
5. **Run, measure, look.** Execute `weld_plug.py`, `inspect_stl.py`, and `render_views.py`. Check actual output size in millimetres, one connected watertight body, the joint cross-section, front/side/top/bottom silhouette, and support-sensitive faces. Mesh validity is a minimum, not proof of a good or printable object.
6. **Slice for your printer.** Choose an orientation with stable first-layer body contact. A brim strengthens bed adhesion; it does not fix a top supported only by detachable tree tips. Keep display-face support damage low and inspect the slicer's actual first layer, support contacts and overhangs.
7. **Print one and learn.** Document exact STL checksum, slicer version, material, nozzle, settings, orientation, physical result and any breakage. Only the exact accepted file earns a print-proven label. Replacing the mesh resets that verdict.

The script's central riser and automatic orientation are conveniences for uncomplicated solid shapes. Review them as geometry; do not assume they fit every photo. The project has seen connected meshes whose thin bridges snapped and apparently polished meshes that looked melted after remeshing. The workflow explicitly allows manual modelling and rejection.
